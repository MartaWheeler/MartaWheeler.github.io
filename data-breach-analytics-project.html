Data Breach Analytics

Predicting Which Breaches Become High-Impact Stories

Exploratory analysis and classification modeling on a 339-incident breach-disclosure dataset — examining sector risk, loss severity, and what predicts whether a breach becomes a major, high-visibility story.

Type: Independent analysis project Methods: EDA, feature engineering, classification Tools: Python, scikit-learn, XGBoost, SMOTE

The Dataset

339 disclosed breaches, one question

Each row is a publicly disclosed data breach, with attributes describing the organization, the method of compromise, the scale and sensitivity of the exposure, and — the target variable — whether the incident was flagged as an especially notable, high-profile story.

Entity & sector
Which organization, and which industry — web, healthcare, government, financial, retail, and more
Method
How the breach happened — hacked, poor security, lost device, inside job, "oops!"
Records lost & sensitivity
Scale of the exposure, and how sensitive the exposed data was (email address only vs. full personal or financial detail)
"Interesting story"
Whether the breach was flagged as a notable, high-profile story — the target variable for the modeling stage

Framing the problem

Rather than only describing past breaches, the goal was to test whether disclosed attributes — sector, method, scale, sensitivity — can predict which incidents escalate into major, high-visibility events.

Descriptive Analysis

Frequent isn't the same as severe

Ranking breach methods by how often they occur versus how many records they ultimately cost tells two different stories.

Most frequent method

Hacked, then lost device, poor security, inside job, and "oops!" — hacking is by far the most common cause of disclosed breaches.

Most records lost (total)

Hacked again leads, but poor security jumps to second — well above lost device — despite causing fewer individual incidents.

Where the exposure concentrates

Web has the highest breach count and the most total records lost. Healthcare, government, and financial follow closely in frequency, while government, financial, app, and retail lose the most records overall.

An outlier worth noting

Military shows strong resistance to hacking, but its exposure comes almost entirely through lost devices — pointing to a process and training gap rather than a technical one.

Drivers of a High-Impact Story

Newsworthy breaches share a profile

Only around 20% of disclosed breaches in the dataset were flagged as high-impact — a strongly imbalanced target that shaped the modeling approach that followed.

Scale

More records lost — and especially very large (100M+) exposures — is strongly associated with high-impact stories.

Sensitivity

Breaches exposing full personal or financial detail, not just an email address, were disproportionately "interesting."

Method

"Oops!" and lost-device incidents punched above their weight — nearly half became notable stories, more than hacking did.

Sector

Legal, military, and financial breaches were most likely to become high-profile, even when less frequent overall.

Predictive Modeling

Eight classifiers, three data treatments

Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Naive Bayes, Gradient Boosting, Random Forest, SVM, KNN, and XGBoost were each evaluated across three stages of data preparation.

1

Baseline

Raw features, one-hot encoded categoricals — every model underperformed the 80% no-information baseline.

2

Scaled

Numeric features standardized — a modest improvement, still close to baseline.

3

Scaled + Balanced

SMOTE oversampling to address the roughly 80/20 class imbalance — the best results across the board.

Best result

KNN (k = 3) on scaled, balanced data. Accuracy landed roughly at the 80% no-information baseline — matching, not beating, the "guess the majority class" floor. Precision and recall on the minority ("interesting") class remained weak, meaning the signal in these features alone wasn't strong enough for reliable production use on its own.

Takeaways

From analysis to action

Where to focus security investment

  • Prioritize hacking defenses first — highest frequency and highest total loss
  • Close the "poor security" gap second — fewer incidents, outsized record loss
  • Pair technical controls with device-loss and human-error training, where "oops!" and lost-device incidents drove outsized reputational impact

Next steps for the model

  • Grow the labeled dataset — 339 rows and a 20% minority class limit what any classifier can learn
  • Add features via NLP on the underlying breach-story text, rather than relying only on derived categorical columns
  • Explore unsupervised clustering to surface breach archetypes the label alone doesn't capture
